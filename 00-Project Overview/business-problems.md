# Business Problems

This section describes the problems this system is designed to solve. Naming them plainly keeps the
project honest: every standard, template, and process step later in this document should trace back to
one of these problems.

## The problems we are solving

1. **Inconsistent email design.** Campaigns have looked and behaved differently depending on who built
   them and when. Spacing, typography, button styles, and layout vary between sends and between brands,
   which erodes trust and makes the brands look less professional than they are.

2. **Repeated campaign themes and messaging.** Without a plan, sends drift into repeating the same
   angle, headline, and hero treatment week after week. Customers notice repetition, and it trains them
   to ignore us.

3. **Duplicated work.** The same header, footer, product grid, and coupon block have been rebuilt from
   scratch again and again. Effort that should be spent once is spent every week.

4. **Repeated mistakes.** The same issues recur — oversized images that break on mobile, unverified
   coupons, out-of-stock products, broken links — because the lessons were never written down where the
   next person (or tool) would see them.

5. **Knowledge trapped in one person's head.** Critical know-how — which product source to trust, how a
   brand's logo should sit, what a "good" send looks like — has lived with individuals rather than in
   shared documentation. This is a single point of failure.

6. **No campaign planning system.** Campaigns have been produced reactively, close to send date, rather
   than planned against a calendar of seasons, holidays, and business priorities. Reactive production is
   slower, more stressful, and lower quality.

7. **Hard to scale across brands.** Each new brand multiplied the manual effort because nothing was
   truly shared. Growth made the problem worse instead of better.

8. **Quality depends on time available, not on standard.** When a send is rushed, quality drops, because
   the quality bar was informal. There was no gate that a campaign had to pass before going out.

## Why these matter to the business

These are not cosmetic concerns. Inconsistency and repetition reduce open and click rates; duplicated
work and rework inflate production cost and delay sends; trapped knowledge creates risk; and the absence
of planning means we miss the high-intent moments (holidays, seasons, EOFY) that drive the most revenue.
Left unaddressed, each new brand and each new campaign type makes the situation harder, not easier.

and [Success Metrics](success-metrics.md) defines how we will know we have.
