# Campaign Planning Framework

Every campaign should be planned before it is built. This framework defines the brief we complete for
each send. It forces the "why" to be answered before the "how", keeps campaigns distinct from one
another, and gives the reviewer and approver a clear standard to check against.

## The campaign brief

Complete these fields before design begins. One primary objective, one primary action.

| Field | What it answers | Notes |
|-------|-----------------|-------|
| **Campaign goal** | Why are we sending this? | One sentence. Tie to a business goal or calendar moment. |
| **Campaign type** | Which type is this? | From [Campaign Strategy](campaign-strategy.md) (weekly, holiday, seasonal, launch, clearance, educational, appreciation, awareness). |
| **Target audience** | Who is this for? | The brand's customers, or a defined segment. |
| **Main offer** | What is the core value or hook? | The single most important thing. May be an offer, a new product, or a theme. |
| **Featured products** | What appears in the grid? | Verified in stock, on-brand, on-theme (see [Campaign Requirements](Campaign-Requirements.md)). |
| **Hero product** | What leads the campaign? | The one product or idea the hero communicates. |
| **Campaign theme** | What is the unifying idea and tone? | Must be fresh vs recent sends. |
| **Primary CTA** | The one action we most want | e.g. "Shop the range". Appears above the fold. |
| **Secondary CTA** | An optional supporting action | Must serve a *different* purpose/destination than the primary (no duplicate CTAs). |
| **Expected customer emotion** | What should they feel? | See [Campaign Psychology](campaign-psychology.md). |
| **Expected customer action** | What should they do? | Click, browse, buy, read, redeem, save for later. |
| **Success criteria** | How will we judge it? | Tie to [Success Metrics](success-metrics.md) (e.g. open rate, CTR, revenue vs baseline). |
| **Risk assessment** | What could go wrong? | e.g. offer not yet active, stock risk, deadline clash, deliverability, brand-fit. |

## How to use the brief

1. **Plan from the calendar.** Start with the fixed anchors (holidays, seasons, EOFY) from the
   [Content Calendar](content-calendar.md) and the [Holiday Framework](holiday-campaign-framework.md).
2. **Fill the brief.** Keep it to a single page. If a field can't be answered, that is a signal the
   campaign isn't ready.
3. **Check for freshness.** Compare theme, hero, and headline against recent sends; a new send must not
   reuse the previous week's angle.
4. **Confirm the risky items early.** Especially: is the coupon created and active? are the products in
   stock? are the dates right?
5. **Hand to design and review.** The completed brief is the input to production and the checklist the
   reviewer and approver sign off against.

## Why a planning framework

A short, consistent brief prevents the two most expensive failure modes: building the wrong thing, and
finding a blocker (dead coupon, out-of-stock hero) only at QA. It also creates a paper trail that makes
each campaign reviewable and each decision explainable — which feeds the
[Decision Log](../09-Architecture%20Decisions/Decision-Log.md).
