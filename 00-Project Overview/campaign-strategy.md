# Campaign Strategy

This section explains the philosophy behind our campaigns: the *kinds* of campaigns we run, **when**
each should be used, **why**, and the **customer behaviour** we expect each to drive. It is a planning
reference for the Campaign Manager and the wider team, not a build specification.

## Philosophy

Every campaign should earn its place in the inbox. Before we send, we should be able to answer: *why
this, why now, and what do we want the customer to do?* Campaigns fall into a small number of repeatable
**types**. Knowing the type sets the tone, the offer logic, the product mix, and the design direction —
which is what makes production fast and consistent.

Two principles run through all types:

- **Right message, right moment.** Match the campaign to what the customer is likely thinking about at
  that point in the calendar or their relationship with us.
- **Value before ask.** Lead with something useful or desirable (products, help, a genuine offer) before
  asking for the click or the purchase.

## Campaign types

| Type | When to use | Why (business rationale) | Expected customer behaviour |
|------|-------------|--------------------------|-----------------------------|
| **Weekly campaign** | Every week, as the always-on drumbeat | Keep the brand present, surface fresh product, drive steady baseline revenue | Browse a curated selection, click through to explore, incremental purchases |
| **Monthly campaign** | Once a month, broader story | Step back from product-of-the-week to a theme, category, or brand message | Re-engage, discover a category, higher consideration purchases |
| **Holiday campaign** | Fixed calendar dates (see [Holiday Framework](holiday-campaign-framework.md)) | Capture high-intent, high-traffic shopping moments | Time-bound, gift- or deal-driven purchases; higher urgency |
| **Seasonal campaign** | Start/turn of a season | Align range with seasonal needs (e.g. winter safety, summer mobility) | Needs-based purchasing tied to the time of year |
| **Product launch** | When a new or hero product arrives | Build awareness and desire for a specific product | Learn about the product, click to the product page, early adoption |
| **Clearance / sale** | End of range, overstock, or planned promotion | Move stock, reward deal-seekers, protect margin elsewhere | Fast, price-led purchasing; urgency and scarcity |
| **Educational** | When the range benefits from explanation | Build trust and confidence, reduce purchase hesitation (especially considered/assistive products) | Read, learn, save for later, return with confidence |
| **Customer appreciation** | Periodically; loyalty moments | Strengthen relationship, reward existing customers, improve retention | Feel valued, re-engage, redeem a thank-you offer |
| **Brand awareness** | When establishing or repositioning a brand | Communicate who the brand is and what it stands for | Recognise and remember the brand; softer, non-transactional response |

## Choosing the right campaign

- Start from the **calendar and the business goal**, not the product. The holiday and seasonal calendar
  (see [Content Calendar](content-calendar.md)) sets the fixed anchors; fill the gaps with weekly,
  educational, and appreciation sends.
- **One primary objective per send.** A campaign that tries to launch a product, clear stock, and
  educate all at once does none well. Pick the type, then let it govern tone, offer, and layout.
- **Vary the type week to week.** Alternating types (product, educational, seasonal, appreciation) keeps
  the programme fresh and avoids the repetition problem named in [Business Problems](business-problems.md).
- **Match copy length to type.** Normal weekly and clearance sends stay concise and scannable; seasonal,
  holiday, educational, and appreciation sends may justify longer, warmer, more emotional copy.

## How campaigns should evolve over time

- Establish the always-on weekly and monthly cadence first (reliable baseline).
- Layer the holiday and seasonal calendar on top so the year is planned, not reactive.
- Introduce educational and appreciation sends to balance the purely promotional ones and support
  retention.
- As measurement matures (see [Success Metrics](success-metrics.md)), let performance data reshape the
  mix — do more of what works for each brand and audience.
