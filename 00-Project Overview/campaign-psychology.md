# Campaign Psychology

Good campaigns are built on an understanding of the customer, not just the product. This section is a
marketing reference for the team: what we want customers to **feel**, **think**, and **do**, and the
design choices that follow from it.

## Feel → Think → Do

Every campaign should be designed against three questions, in order:

1. **What should the customer FEEL?**
   Emotion comes first because it drives attention and memory. Depending on the campaign type, the target
   feeling might be reassurance and confidence (assistive/considered products), excitement and urgency
   (sales), warmth and gratitude (appreciation, gifting), or optimism (new year, seasonal).

2. **What should the customer THINK?**
   The rational follow-through to the feeling: *"This is right for me / for someone I care about", "This
   brand is trustworthy and local", "This is a genuine deal", "This will make daily life easier."* Trust
   signals (Australian owned, fast shipping, support, NDIS registration where relevant) support the
   thought.

3. **What should the customer DO?**
   One clear action, made obvious and easy: shop the range, explore a category, read and learn, or redeem
   an offer. The primary CTA must be unmistakable and reachable above the fold.

If a campaign can't answer all three, it isn't ready.

## Emotion in the hero banner

The hero is the first impression and carries most of the emotional load. Every hero should:

- **Communicate a feeling at a glance**, before any copy is read — through imagery, tone, and composition.
- **Show the outcome, not just the object.** A person enjoying a day out communicates more than a product
  on a white background.
- **Set up the theme** the rest of the email supports, without over-crowding the top.
- **Lead the eye to the primary action** (brand → hook → concise message → CTA/products).

## Lifestyle photography vs catalogue images

Both have a place; choose deliberately.

- **Lifestyle photography** (people, real settings, outcomes) is stronger for **emotional and
  aspirational** moments — the hero of a seasonal, appreciation, brand-awareness, or "everyday outings"
  campaign. It answers *"what does my life look like with this?"* and builds desire and trust.
- **Catalogue / product images** (clean, consistent, on white) are stronger for the **product grid and
  comparison** — where the job is clarity, accurate representation, and easy scanning across many items.

As a rule: **lead with lifestyle to create feeling, support with catalogue to enable decisions.** Do not
swap approved lifestyle creative for plain product shots merely to save effort — that trades away the
emotional hook (see the image rules in `CLAUDE.md`).

## Practical cues

- **Reduce hesitation** for considered purchases with reassurance: support, returns, warranty, local
  service. This matters most for brands serving older or care-focused customers.
- **Create honest urgency** for sales with real deadlines and limited offers — never manufactured scarcity.
- **Respect the reader.** Concise, scannable copy for normal sends; warmth and length only where the
  moment earns it. Accessibility (legible sizes, contrast) is part of respect and directly affects
  conversion for some audiences.
